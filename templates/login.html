What happens to your data

  • Each mood you type is sent to OpenAI with your answers to the taste questions and a summary of your Spotify listening, so its model can suggest songs. The summary covers your most played artists and tracks, what you played recently, some of your liked songs, the artists you follow and your playlists' names. Up to 150 songs you know are listed too, so some can go into your playlists.
  • Signing in with Google gives Moodify your Google email address. It's kept in this browser's login cookie, which expires 30 days after your last visit, and isn't saved anywhere else.
  • Moodify stores your Spotify ID and name, a login token and which Spotify permissions you gave, your answers, that summary and song list (refreshed daily), and the playlists it made for you with their tracks.
  • It can read your listening history, liked songs, followed artists and playlists; create playlists in your account; add songs to your liked songs when you like them in Moodify; and, when you press Play now, add songs to your queue.
  • Disconnect, at the bottom of the home page, deletes everything Moodify stored about you at once. To remove its access to Spotify as well, go to spotify.com/account/apps.
