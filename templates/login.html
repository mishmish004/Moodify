Playlists for the mood you're in

Describe how you feel or what you're doing. Moodify picks songs that fit, in the style you like, and saves them as a private playlist in your Spotify account.

{# POST, not a link: starting the Spotify login from a GET lets prefetchers and crawlers use up the one-time state #}

Friends only: your Spotify account has to be added to the app first. Ask whoever sent you the link.

What happens to your data

  • Each mood you type is sent to OpenAI with your answers to the taste questions and a summary of your Spotify listening, so its model can suggest songs. The summary covers your most played artists and tracks, what you played recently, some of your liked songs, the artists you follow and your playlists' names. Up to 150 songs you know are listed too, so some can go into your playlists.
  • Moodify stores your Spotify ID and name, a login token and which Spotify permissions you gave, your answers, that summary and song list (refreshed daily), the playlists it made for you with their tracks, and the songs you like and the 1–10 ratings you give in Moodify.
  • When you write a review of a playlist, it's sent to OpenAI with that playlist's tracks, your likes and rating, so its model can work out what to do differently. What it learns (a few short lessons, each quoting your review) is sent along with your next requests, together with the songs you liked and what you asked for in playlists you rated 8 or more. Reviews, their earlier versions and the lessons are kept until you delete them or disconnect; "Memory" shows all of it and lets you delete any part.
  • It can read your listening history, liked songs, followed artists and playlists; create playlists in your account; add songs to your liked songs when you like them in Moodify (you can turn this off in Settings); and, when you press Play now, add songs to your queue.
  • The songs in your library (what you play, like and keep) are sent to OpenAI to be described: energy, mood, how electronic or danceable they are, decade, genres and language. Moodify keeps those descriptions with the songs, and they go when the songs leave your library.
  • Nothing about you is shown to or used for other people on Moodify unless you switch it on under Friends → Sharing settings. Even then, what you type, your reviews, ratings and lessons are never shared.
  • Disconnect, at the bottom of the home page, deletes everything Moodify stored about you at once. To remove its access to Spotify as well, go to spotify.com/account/apps.
